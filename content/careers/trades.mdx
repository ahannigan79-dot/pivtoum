**Does it have to be done in person, with your hands? — and why "manual work" is the wrong category**

This factor is usually described as though it were binary: desk jobs are exposed, manual jobs are safe. That framing is wrong, and the trades are where it breaks most visibly.

What actually matters is not whether the work is physical. It is whether the *environment* is predictable.

Robotics performs well in controlled settings — known dimensions, repeated tasks, fixed tolerances, no surprises. That is a warehouse, an assembly line, a modular construction facility. It performs badly in uncontrolled ones, and the gap is not narrowing quickly.

A service electrician spends the day in unpredictable environments by definition: a different building every job, unlabeled wiring, work someone else did badly in 1974, a customer describing the problem inaccurately. That rates 9.5.

The same trade skills applied to a production line rate 8.0 — still physical, but in exactly the setting robotics handles best. That single difference, plus lower trust and licensing requirements, is what separates a 2.5 from a 5.0.

**So the useful question for any hands-on career is not "is this manual work?" but would two days of this job ever look the same?** If the answer is no, the protection is real. If the answer is yes, physical work offers much less shelter than people assume.

## Which trades are safest from AI?

Ranked by exposure, safest first:

1. **Electrical service and maintenance — 2.5.** Unpredictable environments, licensed, high trust, high consequence.
2. **Plumbing and HVAC service — 2.6.** Same shape, marginally less regulated in some jurisdictions.
3. **Welding and fabrication on site — 3.2.** Physical and skilled, but with more repeatable work and weaker licensing.
4. **Construction site trades — 3.4.** Genuinely varied work, though modular methods are shifting some of it indoors.
5. **Production line and prefab — 5.0.** Structured, repeatable, and the setting where automation advances fastest.

## What this means if you're already in the field

There isn't much of a defensive move to make here — the score is already about as low as this index goes, and the direction of travel is toward you, not away. The exposed edge is the structured, repeatable end: production-line and prefab work rates 5.0 because it sits in exactly the controlled setting robotics handles best. Everything done in an unpredictable environment — a different building every job, unlabeled wiring, work someone else did badly in 1974 — is where your protection is real.

If anything the ground is getting firmer. Analysis of job postings found skilled-trades demand grew 27% over four years, outpacing desk-based professional roles by 19 percentage points — over the same period that unemployment among recent college graduates hit its highest level in more than a decade outside the pandemic. Electrical work in particular sits directly in front of the electrification and data-center buildout: the one trade with a demand tailwind coming from AI itself.

**The honest other side has nothing to do with technology** — physical wear over thirty years and income ceilings outside business ownership are real, and our index measures automation exposure, not whether a body lasts the distance.
